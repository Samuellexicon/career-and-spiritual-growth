A little about my journey

Growing in faith and in my work

As I continue my education and work toward my professional goals, I have come to understand that personal growth is about more than gaining knowledge, earning qualifications, or finding a good job. It is also about becoming a better person, strengthening my relationship with Heavenly Father, and learning how to use my abilities to help others.

My journey from electrical maintenance into Information Technology has required me to learn new things, adjust to challenges, and remain committed to my goals. Through these experiences, I have learned the importance of patience, hard work, faith, and continuous learning.

This portfolio brings together the spiritual values I want to live by and the professional responsibilities I hope to carry with integrity.

The foundation

My three finalized guiding principles

1. Keeping Jesus Christ at the Center

The first principle guiding my spiritual and professional portfolio is the conviction that Jesus Christ must remain at the center of every decision, ambition, and responsibility I carry. It is easy to become so consumed by education, work, and planning for the future that the most important things are gradually displaced by the most urgent ones. I want to develop and maintain the habit of turning to Heavenly Father for guidance, especially when facing difficult decisions or unexpected challenges, and to remember that being a successful IT professional is not only about technical knowledge. It is also about being honest, humble, dependable, and trustworthy. The kind of professional I want to become is one whose colleagues and clients know they can rely on him to do what is right, not just what is efficient or convenient.

This principle is grounded in the Savior’s teaching in 3 Nephi 13:19–22, which calls us to focus on what has lasting value rather than becoming too attached to temporary achievements or possessions. I understand this passage as a direct call to keep my priorities in the right order and ensure that my professional ambitions never take the place of my relationship with God. The video on Jesus Christ as the Prince of Peace reinforced this by reminding me that He can bring genuine peace and stability during difficult seasons, and that I do not have to face professional or personal challenges without spiritual grounding and divine guidance.

2. Lifelong Learning, Personal Growth, and Diligence

My second guiding principle is to never stop learning or working toward becoming a better version of myself, spiritually and professionally. Growth takes time, and there will always be new things to learn, challenges to overcome, and opportunities to improve. My educational journey has reinforced this conviction in a personal way. The transition from electrical maintenance into Information Technology pushed me well outside my comfort zone. I had to develop entirely new knowledge, build confidence in unfamiliar technologies, and trust that consistent effort over time would produce competence. It did, but only because I did not wait for circumstances to be ideal before beginning.

I also believe that learning cannot stop after graduation. Technology continues to evolve, and I will need to keep developing my skills throughout my career. More importantly, I want to continue learning spiritually, to become someone who accepts correction with humility, grows through experience, and follows the direction of the Lord in every phase of life. Doctrine and Covenants 58:27 captures this principle precisely by calling us to be anxiously engaged rather than passive, taking responsibility for our growth rather than waiting for it to happen to us.

3. Serving Others through Integrity, Resilience, and Christlike Love

My third guiding principle is to use what I learn and the abilities I develop to make a positive difference in the lives of others. Life is not only about what I achieve for myself but about how I treat people and the contributions I make. Whether at home, in the workplace, at school, or in the community, I want to be someone who is willing to help, who listens carefully, and who treats others with consistent kindness and respect. This principle is especially important in my chosen career. Working in IT involves more than maintaining servers, configuring networks, or managing cloud infrastructure. Behind every technical issue is a person or an organization depending on those systems to work properly. I want to approach every professional responsibility with honesty, patience, and a genuine willingness to help.

Mosiah 2:17 teaches that serving other people is also a way of serving God, and that even simple acts of support and service carry lasting meaning. That scripture has become a professional compass for me. When I am deciding how to respond to a difficult client situation, how to handle a security incident that affects real people’s data, or how to treat a colleague who is struggling, the question I want to ask is not what is most efficient but what does service look like here. My faith is not separate from my professional identity. It is the foundation it rests on.

Personal reflection

Notes and insights from my reflection activity

During my reflection activity, the thought that stood out most clearly to me was this: I have been building longer than I realized. When I looked honestly at the journey from electrical maintenance at Volta Aluminium to IT systems administration, I did not see a career change. I saw a single continuous thread of learning to maintain systems that people depend on. The motors and switchgear I have maintained for over a decade and the cloud infrastructure and Linux servers I am now learning to build are different technologies, but they require the same foundational qualities: precision, accountability, patience under pressure, and the humility to keep learning. That realization brought a quiet sense of peace that I did not expect. I also thought about how easy it is to become so focused on achieving a goal that we forget to appreciate the journey and the lessons being learned along the way. Between education, work, and planning for the future, there are many responsibilities competing for attention, and I want to be more intentional about making time for spiritual things and seeking Heavenly Father’s guidance in the decisions I make.

Spiritually, what developed most during this reflection was my understanding of Elder Bednar’s intertwined strands analogy. I had understood it intellectually before, but sitting quietly with it this time I felt it more personally. My faith has not been separate from my professional journey. It has been woven through it. The discipline of prayer that helped me stay grounded during difficult coursework, the covenant-keeping that kept me honest when shortcuts were available, and the service orientation that shaped how I treated clients during my internship at Pasovent were not additions to my professional development. They were part of its structure. Remove any one of those strands and the rope would have been weaker. Going into my IT career, I intend to carry this integrated identity deliberately. I want to be a systems administrator whose technical competence is inseparable from his integrity, and whose first question in any professional role is not what can I accomplish but how can I serve the people depending on these systems. The temple videos reminded me that the blessings available through covenants are real and present, not distant or theoretical, and that I cannot take for granted the spiritual habits that have made this season of growth possible. That is the confidence and the commitment I want to bring into every professional environment I enter.

Looking ahead

My commitment moving forward

I want to keep Jesus Christ at the center of my decisions, remain committed to learning and personal improvement, and use my abilities to serve others with honesty and compassion.

I know I still have much to learn, both spiritually and professionally. I intend to carry the lessons from my past experience into my future in IT, remembering that technical competence matters most when it is joined with integrity and a sincere desire to serve.
