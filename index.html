A little about my journey

Growing in faith and in my work

As I continue my education and work toward my professional goals, I have come to understand that personal growth is about more than gaining knowledge, earning qualifications, or finding a good job. It is also about becoming a better person, strengthening my relationship with Heavenly Father, and learning how to use my abilities to help others.

My journey has not always followed a straight path. Moving from electrical maintenance into Information Technology has required me to learn new things, adjust to challenges, and remain committed to my goals. Through these experiences, I have learned the importance of patience, hard work, faith, and continuous learning.

As I develop this portfolio, I want to show how my spiritual beliefs and professional ambitions can work together. The three principles below are reminders of the direction I want my life to take.

The foundation

My three guiding principles

1. Keeping Jesus Christ at the Center of My Life

One of the most important things I want to remember as I move forward in life is to keep Jesus Christ at the center of everything I do. It is easy to become so focused on education, work, financial responsibilities, and future plans that I sometimes forget to make time for the things that matter most spiritually.

I want to develop the habit of turning to Heavenly Father for guidance, especially when making important decisions or facing difficult situations. I believe that having faith in Jesus Christ can help me remain strong, make better choices, and find peace even when things do not go as planned.

This principle also influences the kind of professional I want to become. As I continue developing my skills in Information Technology and System Administration, I want to remember that being successful is not just about technical knowledge. It is also about being honest, humble, dependable, and respectful toward others. I want people to know that they can trust me and rely on me to do what is right.

Scripture: 3 Nephi 13:19–22. This passage reminds me to focus on what has lasting value instead of becoming too attached to temporary things. I understand it as a reminder to keep my priorities in the right order and make sure that my ambitions do not take the place of my relationship with God.

2. Lifelong Learning, Personal Growth, and Diligence

My second guiding principle is to never stop learning or working toward becoming a better version of myself. One thing life has taught me is that growth takes time. There will always be new things to learn, challenges to overcome, and opportunities to improve.

My educational journey has helped me appreciate this principle. Moving from electrical maintenance into Information Technology has pushed me outside my comfort zone. I have had to develop new knowledge, understand unfamiliar technologies, and become more confident in my abilities.

Through my studies at Ensign College and BYU-Pathway Worldwide, I have continued building my knowledge in technical support, networking, and system administration. These experiences have taught me that progress comes through consistency, patience, and a willingness to learn from mistakes.

I also believe that learning should not stop after graduation. Technology continues to change, and I will need to keep developing my skills throughout my career. More importantly, I want to continue learning spiritually and become someone who is willing to accept correction, grow through experience, and follow the direction of the Lord.

Scripture: Doctrine and Covenants 58:27. This scripture encourages us to be actively involved in doing good rather than waiting for everything to happen on its own. It reminds me to take responsibility for my growth, make good use of my opportunities, and put effort into the goals I want to achieve.

3. Serving Others Through Integrity, Resilience, and Christlike Love

My third guiding principle is to use what I learn and the abilities I develop to make a positive difference in the lives of others.

I believe that life is not only about what I achieve for myself but also about how I treat people and the contributions I make. Whether at home, in the workplace, at school, or in the community, I want to be someone who is willing to help, listen, and treat others with kindness and respect.

This principle is especially important in my chosen career. Working in Information Technology involves more than maintaining computers, configuring networks, or managing systems. Behind every technical issue is a person or an organization depending on those systems to work properly.

As I prepare for a career in IT and System Administration, I want to approach my responsibilities with honesty, patience, and a willingness to help. I know that challenges and mistakes are part of professional life, but I want to learn from them instead of giving up. I also want to remain calm under pressure and treat colleagues and users with respect, even when situations become difficult.

My faith reminds me that serving others is one way of showing love for God. I want my professional knowledge to become a tool that allows me to help people and contribute positively wherever I work.

Scripture: Mosiah 2:17. This scripture teaches that serving other people is also a way of serving God. It reminds me that even simple acts of kindness, support, and service can have lasting meaning.

Personal reflection

Connecting spiritual progression with professional growth

As I took time to reflect on my life and the direction I want to take, I thought about the experiences that have brought me to where I am today. I considered my education, work experiences, personal challenges, and the importance of keeping my faith strong while working toward my goals. One thing that stood out to me is that it is possible to become so focused on achieving something that we forget to appreciate the journey and the lessons we are learning along the way.

I also thought about the importance of making time for spiritual things, even when life becomes busy. Between education, work, and planning for the future, there are many responsibilities that compete for my attention. However, I want to be more intentional about my relationship with Heavenly Father and remember to seek His guidance in the decisions I make.

One message that particularly caught my attention was Elder David A. Bednar's analogy of the intertwined strands of a rope. It helped me understand that my spiritual and professional lives do not have to compete with each other. They can work together to make me stronger. My education can develop my knowledge, my work experiences can teach me responsibility, and my faith can help guide the way I use what I learn.

When I think about my transition from electrical maintenance into Information Technology, I recognize how much patience, effort, and willingness to learn have been required. There have been unfamiliar concepts and challenges, but each experience has provided an opportunity to grow. I want to carry these lessons into my future career, particularly as I develop in system administration and other areas of IT.

Professionally, I want to be more than someone who knows how to solve technical problems. I want to be someone who is dependable, honest, willing to help, and respectful toward others. I believe that the values I develop through my faith can make me a better colleague and a more responsible professional.

Overall, this reflection has reminded me that success is not only about earning qualifications or reaching career goals. It is also about the person I become along the way. Moving forward, I want to continue strengthening my faith, being committed to lifelong learning, and finding meaningful ways to serve others. My goal is to build a life in which my spiritual values and professional ambitions support one another, with Jesus Christ remaining at the center.
